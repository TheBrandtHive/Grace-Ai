### 2.3 Conditions (US Dept of State Human Rights Report 2023 — Ghana)

Classification: "Generally harsh"

Documented issues:
- Overcrowding substantially exceeds official capacity across the system
- Medium-security prisons MORE overcrowded than lower-security "camp prisons"
- Inadequate sanitation
- Lack of medical care
- Food inadequate: meals routinely lack fruit, vegetables, or meat
- Prisoners rely on family/community donations to supplement food
- Pretrial detainees: authorities do NOT provide food
  — families must supply; if families cannot, prison officers have paid personally
- Facilities: aging or repurposed public/military buildings despite improvements
  — poor ventilation, substandard construction, inadequate space and lighting

## SECTION 11: MISCELLANEOUS RAW FACTS

Bail:
  Police must bring to court OR release: within 48 hours of arrest
  Bail is FREE — bond amount only enforced if accused absconds
  Types: (1) Police Enquiry Bail; (2) Bail Pending Trial; (3) Bail Pending Appeal
  Section 96(3) Act 30: amount "shall not be excessive or harsh"
  Section 96(5) Act 30: grounds where bail may be refused
  No bail schedule exists in Ghana (unlike USA) — all discretionary per case
  Surety justification: may require proof of assets (e.g., title deed)
  Bail pending appeal: granted AFTER conviction; much harder to obtain

Arrest:
  Private citizen arrest: permitted only for offences involving force/violence,
    bodily harm, theft/fraud, injury to public/private property — witnessed in person
  Police warrant execution: can be executed anywhere in Ghana
  Recapture: person escaping lawful custody may be immediately pursued and arrested
    at any place in Ghana

Marriage registration:
  Customary: within 3 months; at District Assembly or local authority
  Not mandatory for validity; but critical for legal enforceability
  Ordinance: registered by Registrar of Marriages; monogamous only

Inheritance:
  Matrilineal (Akan — Ashanti, Fante): through mother's line
  Patrilineal (Ewe, Ga, most northern groups): through father's line
  Intestate Succession: PNDC Law 111 (statutory framework for dying without a will)

Retirement:
  Mandatory retirement age for all Ghanaian judges: 70 years
  Salary/conditions cannot be reduced while in office (constitutional protection)

Penalty unit: GH₵12.00 per unit (government-set; periodically revised)

Court dress (High Court and above): horsehair wigs + scarlet robes (British tradition retained)

Adversarial system: Ghana courts operate adversarially (both sides compete before
  impartial judge) — not inquisitorial

Martyrs' Day: observed annually on 30 June

Court number reference (Accra Law Court Complex): 44 High Courts in complex
